What I see at PCV.

A founder-led product, with marketing starting from zero.

The VP reports straight to the founder, builds the function from the ground up, and gets a say in product direction, pricing and go-to-market.

A subscription business for job seekers.

A consumer and prosumer job-search tool. The goal is a self-serve funnel that moves people from first visit to paid plan, with targets on acquisition cost, conversion and retention.

Growth comes through other people's audiences.

Career coaches, training programs, universities and outplacement firms already hold job seekers' trust. A B2B2C partner channel is part of the brief, not something to add later.

A company that wants numbers.

Applicants are asked for one growth result they drove: the number, what they did, how long it took. Budget reporting goes to the founder and investors.

The problem to solve

Job seekers are anxious, short on cash, and drowning in free tools. PCV has to earn a monthly payment from them, keep it after they land a job, and reach them through the people they already trust. That's a math problem as much as a messaging one.

The first 90 days.

30days

Measure the funnel

  • Instrument every step from first visit to trial, paid and month-two renewal
  • Interview recent subscribers and recent cancels
  • Set CAC, conversion and payback targets with the founder
  • Test two positioning angles on paid social

60days

Open the partner channel

  • Pilot a tracked partner program with 10–20 career coaches and one outplacement firm
  • Launch onboarding and win-back lifecycle in email and SMS
  • Run capped-CPA paid tests, and plan SEO content around the searches job seekers actually make

90days

Scale what pays back

  • Move budget to the channels that clear payback
  • Pitch the first university career centers with pilot results
  • Hire the first one or two marketers
  • Give investors one dashboard and one budget plan
