Restarting a personal loan engine is a math problem: every new channel has to pay for itself at the funded account, not the application.

You are asking for a 6 to 12 month roadmap to ramp volume and widen eligibility while credit and fraud losses stay in check. I have spent 20+ years being paid only when the customer actually converted. This memo sets out what I see, how my record maps to your requirements, and what I would do in the first 90 days.

1.

What I see at Citizens

  1. (a)A franchise with reach most lenders would pay for. Founded in 1828 in Providence, Citizens runs about 1,000 branches and 3,000 ATMs across 14 states and DC. Branch traffic and existing deposit customers are a pre-qualified audience a fintech has to buy. Source: Citizens 2Q26 earnings release.
  2. (b)A balance sheet that can carry growth. $233.8B in total assets, $185.6B in deposits and $70.2B in retail loans at June 30, 2026, with retail growth so far led by home equity and mortgage. Source: 2Q26 release.
  3. (c)Personal loans are coming back from a standing start. By public reports, Citizens stopped writing personal loans for new applicants a few years ago and kept servicing the existing book. The old product ran $5K to $50K over 3 to 7 years with no origination, late or prepayment fees. A volume ramp means rebuilding demand, partners and the funnel from scratch. Source: third-party lender reviews, 2026.
  4. (d)The mandate is growth with guardrails. The role sits under the Head of Personal Loans and works across Marketing, Product, Risk, Finance, Operations, Analytics and Technology. Volume only counts if the loans fund, perform and pass fraud checks.

A 6 to 12 month plan to grow Personal Loans: new segments, channels and markets; business cases for marketing spend; a multi-channel strategy across digital, email, direct mail, branch and partnerships; better application, approval and funded conversion; and reporting executives trust.

Buy funded accounts, not clicks or applications, at a cost per funded account Finance and Risk will sign off on, while the eligibility box widens.

5.

Recommendation: the first 90 days

  1. Days 1–30

    Build the model everyone signs

    • Map the funnel from offer to funded by source, and find the biggest drop.
    • Agree a funded-account value and a CPA ceiling per channel with Finance, Risk and Pricing.
    • Size the warm base first: deposit customers, branch traffic and past borrowers in good standing.
  2. Days 31–60

    Run small tests with stop-losses

    • Launch pre-qualified offers to existing customers through email, digital and branch.
    • Open two or three partner tests (loan marketplaces and comparison sites) paid on funded loans, with fraud and consent checks at the door.
    • Fix the top funnel leak with Product, starting from the application to approval step.
  3. Days 61–90

    Scale what clears the bar

    • Move budget to the channels under their CPA ceiling and cut the rest.
    • Ship a one-page weekly executive view: volume, approval rate, funded conversion, cost per funded account and early loss signals.
    • Present the 6 to 12 month roadmap with segments, channels and the spend case behind each.

I'd welcome a conversation about how you see the ramp, and where you'd want me to start.
