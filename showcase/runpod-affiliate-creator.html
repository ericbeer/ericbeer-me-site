The brief

What you're asking for

  • Own the affiliate program end to end and bring it in-house as the Advertise Purple and FNF agency contracts wind down
  • Move influencer work from sponsorship fees to signups and revenue inside the affiliate program
  • A repeatable creator-sourcing process, with DevRel as a partner
  • Challenges, giveaways, co-branded content and takeovers
  • Employee and executive content systems
  • A reporting cadence on signups, spend and ROI; contracts and payouts

The problem to solve

Two agencies have been running the partner relationships. When they leave, the relationships, the fraud watch and the payout rhythm have to move without a dip.

At the same time, creators who were paid a flat fee for impressions need a new deal: paid on the developers they send and what those developers spend. Some will love it. Some will walk. The program has to be built so the best ones earn more than before.

A-04System schematic

The engine I'd build

One program, one tracking spine, one deal logic. Every creator, affiliate and employee feeds the same loop, and the money comes back on usage, not on views.

S1 · SourcesCreators, affiliates and employees are one partner base with tiers, not three separate programs. DevRel spots the builders; the program signs them.
S2 · AttributionEvery link runs through PartnerStack. Fraud rules sit at the link, before any money moves, so payouts stay clean when the agencies hand over.
S3 · RevenuePay on spend, not views. A creator whose audience keeps building on Runpod should out-earn any flat sponsorship fee, and that is the pitch to them.

A-05Spec callouts

Requirement → proof

R1

End-to-end program ownership

Asked: 4+ years
How I think

Owning a program means owning the partner list, the deal terms, the tracking and the payout date. If any one of those sits with someone else, you don't own it yet.

Proof

20+ years, always paid on results. Founder and CEO of Universal Marketing Partners from 2008 to about 2022, an 8-figure performance and affiliate business. Today I run JVC Affiliate Network on Everflow.

R2

Recruiting and managing affiliates and creators

How I think

Recruit for audience fit, then make the first payout fast. A partner who gets paid early keeps sending traffic.

Proof

$100M+ in affiliate commissions generated over my career. 280+ affiliates tracked on JVC. At BeChosen I built a creator-led acquisition program that recruits and trains creators.

R3

Commission and incentive design

How I think

Your ladder runs from credits to cash. I'd test where the rungs sit, how long the window runs for top partners, and bonuses for developers who keep spending, all against payback.

Proof

I designed the 5-level partner commission plan behind AffiliateSchool.ai's 17 digital products, and set CPA and rev-share terms across lead gen at LeadQual.ai.

R4

PartnerStack or Awin

How I think

Every affiliate platform runs on the same parts: tracked links, attribution windows, fraud rules and payout runs. The platform is a tool; the program logic carries over.

Proof

My own network runs on Everflow, not PartnerStack, so I'd be learning its screens, not the craft. 280+ affiliates of tracking, fraud review and payouts already done that way.

R5

Pulling and reading the data yourself

How I think

I'm a math guy. CPC, CPL, CPA, LTV, payback. For Runpod, the number that matters is spend per referred developer over the commission window, against what we paid.

Proof

Scaled a language-learning product from under $10M to over $100M market cap through Facebook arbitrage, where the margin lives in reading the numbers daily.

R6

Fluency in creator culture

How I think

Creators can tell when a deal treats them like a billboard. Give them real hooks, like a build challenge or early access, and let them make it theirs.

Proof

I host the Performance Marketer podcast, run YouTube @ericbeer, and co-wrote Million Dollar Dads and Million Dollar Identity. Creators and UGC are channels I run.

R7

Developer and AI audiences

Preferred
How I think

Developers ignore hype and respond to proof. The content that works shows the deploy, the cost and the result, and DevRel keeps it accurate.

Proof

I haven't marketed a GPU cloud before. I do build in AI: 50+ apps and AI tools shipped, and LeadQual.ai's AI voice agents qualify and route live insurance leads.

A-06Build sequence

The first 90 days

day 0 to day 90
Days 1–30

Foundation

  1. Take over from Advertise Purple and FNF with a partner-by-partner handover list, so no top affiliate goes a payout cycle without a contact.
  2. Call the top affiliates and creators myself. Ask what they earn, what they'd need to send more, and what annoys them.
  3. Audit PartnerStack: fraud signals, dead links, attribution gaps.
  4. Build the weekly report on signups, spend, ROI and spend per referral.
GateProgram fully in-house, nothing dropped, baseline numbers agreed with the Director of Growth.
Days 31–60

Framing

  1. Move sponsored creators to performance deals, with a short bridge period where needed so the good ones don't walk.
  2. Set up the creator-sourcing pipeline with DevRel: who builds in public on AI, who their audience is, who to sign first.
  3. Test commission ladders and incentives against payback.
  4. Run the first creator campaign, a build challenge on Serverless.
GateEvery creator dollar is tied to signups and spend; first test results read out.
Days 61–90

Load and scale

  1. Launch the employee and executive content system with Product Marketing: simple prompts, a weekly rhythm, amplification.
  2. Formalize partner tiers so the best affiliates and creators earn more, faster.
  3. Standardize contracts and payouts so the program runs on a calendar, not on memory.
  4. Present a two-quarter plan with targets built from the first 60 days of data.
GateA program that grows by adding partners, not by adding headcount.
