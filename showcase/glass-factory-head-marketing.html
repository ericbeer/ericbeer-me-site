The business

What I see at Glass Factory

A media company and a manufacturing company sharing one bloodstream. That's rare, and it changes what marketing should be.

You turned the factory floor into content. Glass Factory makes products for fashion brands, pairing AI systems with relationships with factories around the world. Will Lasry, a Montreal-based designer and manufacturing specialist, founded it to make manufacturing more transparent, and the company films documentaries inside garment factories around the world. Fashion audiences now treat the making of clothes as entertainment, and you were early to that.

The audience is already large. By your own posting, Glass Factory reaches 1.5M+ people across YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and its own platform. That content does three jobs at once: it teaches brands, it earns factories' trust, and it makes the supply chain visible.

"Most companies buy an audience and then go looking for a product. You have both. The job is building the bridge between them."

Eric Beer

The product sits right behind the content. By public listings, the platform covers factory discovery and vetting, design-to-production support, and tools brands need on the way: techpack templates, import cost and tariff estimates, trade-show guides and factory profiles. A viewer who watches a factory tour is one click from a brief.

The model rewards conversion, not just reach. Glass Factory has been around since about 2023 by public reports. Its revenue comes from brands that actually produce. So the scoreboard isn't views. It's viewers who become brands, and brands that place orders. That gap between audience and orders is where I do my best work.

Sources: the Glass Factory Head of Marketing posting on Built In NYC, ChinaTalk's episode notes on Will Lasry, and public company listings. Estimates are marked as such.

The brief

What you're asking for

  • One owner for marketing strategy across social, email, paid, community, launches, resources and brand.
  • Content across Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, LinkedIn, Substack, Reddit, email and Discord.
  • The full workflow: idea, brief, production, posting, analysis, plus content calendars.
  • Paid strategy on Meta, TikTok, Google and YouTube.
  • Leading a community manager, editors and contractors, working closely with the founder.

What you're offering

  • Head of Marketing, full time.
  • Remote, hiring in New York; Montreal or New York preferred.
  • Compensation includes an equity component. No salary range is posted.
  • A seat beside the founder at a company with real audience momentum.

The problem to solve

A brand built on audience and content needs one person who owns the whole growth engine.

Right now social, short-form, email, community and paid can each run on their own. The win is making them one system with one scoreboard: brands that move from watching to manufacturing.

The interviewPage 10

Your requirements, his answers

Six questions for Eric Beer

We put the posting's requirements to him directly. Where he's strong, he shows the receipts. Where the role asks for something he hasn't done, he says so.

Have you grown an audience, yours or someone else's?

Both, and I treat it as a distribution asset you can measure. An audience is worth what it does: joins, buys, refers.

Proof: I host the Performance Marketer podcast and the @ericbeer YouTube channel, and run my own social on every major platform. I built the AffiliateSchool.ai Skool community and grew JVC Affiliate Network to 280+ tracked affiliates in its first year.

Have you led marketing, content and growth at a startup?

That's most of my career. I've been the founder who owns the number, and the operator who builds the team around it.

Proof: Founder and CEO of Universal Marketing Partners from 2008 to about 2022, an 8-figure performance and affiliate business with teams from 5–10 up to 15–20. In 2026 I founded LeadQual.ai and BeChosen / Scout.

What's your short-form video strategy?

Volume from many voices, a clear hook library, and every post tied to a next step. At Glass Factory the factory is the star, and creators and brand founders can tell its story at a scale one channel can't.

Proof: I designed BeChosen's creator program, which recruits and trains creators to run company-owned social channels on a retainer-plus-results model. Creators and UGC are a channel I run, not one I'm learning.

Can you manage creative production across video, static, email and ads?

Yes, as the person who sets the brief, the testing plan and the bar. I lead editors and producers; I don't need to be the one in the timeline.

Honest note: my production experience is direct-response creative at scale, not fashion editorial. I was agency of record for RealAge.com (Hearst / Dr. Oz) and Harlequin Book Club, and drove memberships for Disney Book Club. Your editors bring the craft; I bring the system that tells them what's working.

How do you think about paid social, creative testing and lifecycle email?

I'm a math guy. CPC, CPL, CPA, LTV and payback, read together. Paid finds people, email and community keep them, and the content you already make is the cheapest creative pool anyone has.

Proof: I scaled a language-learning product from under $10M to over $100M market cap through Facebook arbitrage, and drove customers for LifeLock, Norton Antivirus and Credit Sesame. I lead media buyers rather than sitting in Ads Manager myself, and I run owned email and SMS across my businesses.

The posting lists a long tool stack. Where do you stand?

Tools follow the plan. What matters is clean tracking from first view to placed order, so every channel competes on the same scoreboard.

Proof: I've shipped 50+ apps and AI tools and use AI assistants every day. I ran JVC's affiliate tracking on Everflow, and LeadQual.ai routes live leads with consent-compliant tracking. Your stack (Meta, Google, YouTube Studio, Klaviyo, analytics) is familiar ground for my teams.

What's next

Ninety days to turn a big audience into a growth engine with one scoreboard, built alongside Will and the team.

30days

Listen, then measure

  • Sit with Will, the community manager and the editors. Learn the voice before touching it.
  • Audit every channel, then map the path from view to sign-up to brief to order.
  • Fix tracking so YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, email and Discord report against the same goal.
  • Pick the five content formats that already pull brands into the platform.

60days

Build the machine

  • Launch one shared calendar and briefing template from idea to post.
  • Start paid tests on Meta and TikTok using cut-downs of your best factory videos.
  • Build lifecycle email that walks new subscribers from curious to briefing.
  • Pilot a creator program: brand founders and fashion creators telling their factory stories.

90days

Scale what works

  • Put budget behind winning creative, with clear CPA and payback targets.
  • Make community a channel: Discord and Substack programming that feeds the platform.
  • Plan launches and resource drops on a quarterly cadence.
  • Hand Will a one-page monthly report: audience, sign-ups, briefs, orders.
