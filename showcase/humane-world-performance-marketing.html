thesis

A $13M media budget that returns $4M in year one is a lifetime-value bet. The sustainers are the whole game.

You aren't hiring someone to spend $13M. You're hiring someone to buy 40,000 people who keep giving, and to prove which channels found them. That's direct response with a long tail, and it's the work I've done for 20+ years, always paid on results.

Membership math, not media math.

What I see at Humane World for Animals

observed
  • One name, one global brand, since February 2025. The Humane Society of the United States and Humane Society International unified as Humane World for Animals, working in 50+ countries. A rebrand that young means a lot of donors still search for the old name, and paid search has to meet them there. Source: Humane World press release and The NonProfit Times, Feb 2025.
  • A contribution-funded organization at real scale. Total revenue of about $216.5M in fiscal 2025, roughly $181.4M of it contributions. Individual givers are the engine, so acquisition cost and retention move the whole budget. Source: Form 990 via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer.
  • Every channel at once. The plan spans search, display, social, DRTV, CTV, podcasts and third-party list buys, plus a global social program across 100+ handles. That's a portfolio to allocate, not a channel to run. Source: the job posting.
  • Investigations, rescues and policy wins make the creative. Factory farming, the fur trade, puppy mills, animal testing and wildlife cruelty give you emotionally true stories, which is exactly what direct-response creative needs to convert cold audiences.

The brief, as I read it

from the posting

What you're asking for

A leader for paid media and performance: donor acquisition and retention, blended attribution, testing, audience research, a multi-year growth plan across digital, TV and audio, external partners, the global social program and a growing team.

What you're offering

  • $115,000 to $185,000 a year, full time
  • Remote in the US, Eastern Time hours as needed
  • A $13M+ budget and a mission worth the work

The problem to solve

Hit hard acquisition numbers across a dozen channels while proving, in a way Finance and Philanthropy both trust, which dollars actually found donors who stay.

$13M+paid media budget
40Knew donors
11Knew sustainers
$4Mfirst-year revenue

Back-of-napkin, from the posted numbers

$13M ÷ 40,000 donors ≈ $325 spent per donor
$4M ÷ 40,000 donors ≈ $100 back per donor in year one

If the whole budget went to acquisition (it may not), year one runs about $3 out for every $1 in. That's fine when sustainers stay for years. So the metric I'd manage to is cost per retained sustainer and 24-month value by channel, not cost per first gift.

The first 90 days

a checklist page

Days 1 to 30

Listen and get the numbers straight
  • Sit with Brand, Digital, Acquisition, Philanthropy, Data Analytics and Finance; write down how each defines a win
  • Rebuild last year's spend by channel against first gifts, sustainers and 12-month value
  • Audit every agency and list partner: what they're paid on and what they report
  • Check paid search on the old "Humane Society" names and the new brand
  • Walk the donation funnel on mobile and log every leak

Days 31 to 60

Agree on the scoreboard
  • Ship one blended attribution view that Finance signs off on
  • Set a target cost per sustainer and a 24-month value by channel
  • Launch donation-page tests: monthly default, ask ladders, form length
  • Run a geo or audience holdout on one large channel to check incrementality
  • Brief the social team on paid amplification across the 100+ handles

Days 61 to 90

Reallocate and plan the years ahead
  • Move budget toward channels that produce sustainers who stay
  • Pilot one new partner channel, paid on donor quality, with a kill-or-scale date
  • Restructure agency scopes and fees around the shared scoreboard
  • Present the multi-year growth and retention plan with budget by channel
  • Set up the team: roles, weekly reviews and a testing calendar
