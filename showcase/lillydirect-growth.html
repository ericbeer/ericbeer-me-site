LillyDirect built the front door. Now it needs the growth engine behind it.

The platform has more partners, more products and lower prices than ever. The open question is which channels bring new patients who stay on therapy, and which only move the ones Lilly already had.

By Eric Beer, performance marketer, 20+ years paid on results. Candidate for Senior Director, LillyDirect Marketing Strategy & Growth Acceleration.

When LillyDirect opened in January 2024, it was a simple idea: let a patient reach Lilly medicines, an independent telehealth prescriber and home delivery in one place. Two months in, CEO David Ricks said the company was "having trouble keeping up" with demand.

Since then the platform has become a growth business. Self-pay Zepbound vials arrived in August 2024. By February 2025, Lilly said roughly one in ten new patients starting treatment in the obesity market was coming through LillyDirect's self-pay pharmacy.

Then the map got crowded, in a good way. Under Lilly's November 2025 agreement with the U.S. government, Zepbound on LillyDirect starts at $299 and orforglipron at $149. Foundayo was approved on April 1, 2026, and now sells through LillyDirect, TrumpRx and a GoodRx partnership reaching 70,000+ pharmacies.

That is exactly the moment a growth strategist earns their keep. Every new door is a new channel, and every channel has a cost per patient, a refill curve and an overlap with the others. Without one scorecard across consumer and HCP, budget follows whoever shouts loudest.

My job, as I read the posting, is to set that scorecard, run a disciplined pipeline of tests, scale the few that work and shut down the rest, and make co-marketing deals pay their way. That is the work I've done for 20 years, in categories where I was only paid when it worked.

What I see at LillyDirect

Four dispatches from the public record

The model

A manufacturer that sells to the patient

LillyDirect connects patients to independent telehealth providers and dispenses through pharmacy partners such as Amazon Pharmacy and Truepill. Lilly owns the relationship that a retail pharmacy used to hold.

The price lever

Self-pay is the growth story

Vials at $349 to $499 a month, cut further to $299 for Zepbound, made cash pay a real path for patients without coverage. Lilly has tied lower prices to refills within 45 days, which is a retention program.

The portfolio

A second front door in a pill

Foundayo, an oral GLP-1 with no food or water timing rules, starts at $149. It reaches a different consumer than an injection does, and it needs its own funnel and message.

The partners

Many doors, one patient

GoodRx, TrumpRx, telehealth brands and Amazon all now sell or route Lilly medicines. Each partnership is a channel with its own economics, and they overlap. Someone has to own the incrementality question.

The problem to solve

Spend where it adds patients

LillyDirect has demand, partners and price. What it needs is a single growth system across consumer and HCP that says which channels and partners add new patients who stay on therapy.

Then the discipline to fund those, retire the rest, and turn every co-marketing deal into something measurable.

Coming up: the first 90 days

Day 30

Learn and baseline

  • Sit with the AVP, BD, HCP, consumer experience and pharmacy teams; learn the review and compliance path
  • Build one channel scorecard: cost per new patient, 45-day refill rate, payback, by channel and partner
