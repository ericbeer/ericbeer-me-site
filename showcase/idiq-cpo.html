01 / What I see at IDIQ04

I know your category, and your channel.

The business

A portfolio of protection subscriptions under one roof.

IdentityIQ, MyScoreIQ, Credit & Debt and IDIQ Pre-Paid Legal: identity theft protection, credit monitoring, credit reports and legal plans, with repeat Inc. 5000 growth behind them.

The customer

Consumers who sign up worried and stay only if they feel protected.

The moment of purchase is emotional. The reason to keep paying has to be visible every month: alerts that make sense, help when something goes wrong, and a score that moves.

The market

A crowded category where the same promises compete for the same partners.

I've driven customers for LifeLock, Norton and Credit Sesame. I've seen how partners choose which offer to run, and it's rarely the one with the longest feature list.

The model

B2C and B2B2C subscriptions, mostly partner-distributed.

That makes product and distribution one problem. Packaging, pricing and onboarding decide what a partner earns per member, and what a member is worth to IDIQ.

My thesis for IDIQ

In a partner-sold subscription, the product is the growth engine. Activation and retention are the roadmap.
